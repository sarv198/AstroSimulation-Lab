What is happening?

In general relativity, mass curves spacetime, and light follows that curved geometry. For a distant source behind a compact foreground mass, the point-mass lens model predicts one or two images. If the source, lens, and observer line up almost perfectly, the images merge into an Einstein ring.

β = θ − θE2 / θ

β is the source's angular offset, θ is an image's angular position, and θE is the Einstein radius. The simulation solves this simplified lens equation for image positions.

Your mini investigation

Start with near-perfect alignment, then slowly move the source away from the lens center. Watch the ring break into two images and compare their brightness. Next, increase the lens mass and see how the Einstein radius grows.
